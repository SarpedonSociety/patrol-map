DESCRIPTION
Contact was made with the target visually as it emerged from
a squally background, shortly after sunrise.  Sea was calm,
sky almost completely overcast, frequent heavy rain squalls.
Range was closing rapidly, from appearance masts angle on
the bow was nearly zero, submerged before he sighted us and
commenced approach.  Target was later identified as similar
to KAIKWA MARU (ONI 14 P.63) and probably was converted
to an AV as it had only three goalposts instead of four, and
closely resembled the picture shown of the converted AV.

U.S.S. SILVERSIDES - Report of Sixth War Patrol.
TARGET DATA (Cont.)
Closed track at 9 kts. on normal approach course.  While
standing by to fire, target zigged away, making track 150
instead of 90.  Changed setup and commenced firing, using 9
sec. interval and a speed spread of 13 to 16 kts.  First two
torpedoes were prematures, target sighted these and avoided
other two torpedoes.  Target then commenced shooting 5" gun
in our general direction, dropped a "nuisance" depth charge
and headed away from us.  Surfaced with him in sight and
started chase and end around run at full speed.  Target
ducked into a heavy rain squall and contact was not regained.
We searched for him until dusk.  Weather squally all day.

U.S.S. SILVERSIDES                   PATROL NO. 6
TORPEDO ATTACK NO. 2
TIME 0947 K.  DATE August 5, 1943    LAT 01-53 N.  LONG 153-52 E.
TARGET DATA
DESCRIPTION
At 0547 sighted masts of target bearing 021 T,
estimated target course 170 T.  Increased speed to 19 kts
and worked into position dead ahead of target, keeping his
masts in sight.  It was clear and sunny, sea almost a flat
calm.  At 0900 having worked into position ahead, submerged
and started approach.  Set torpedoes at 15 feet.  At 0918
sighted masts through periscope, angle on the bow zero.  By
0934 his angle on the bow was still zero, range decreasing
rapidly and his silhouette resembled a destroyer.  Set tor-
pedoes at 8 feet, changed course to get off the track a
little so could see if he was escorting a larger ship astern
of him.  By 0944 he was identified as a naval auxiliary,
should have reset torpedoes deeper but he was coming fast
and was concentrating on not letting him get by.  At 0947
fired a spread of four from the stern tubes, # 7 (1st) and
#9 (3rd) exploded prematurely #8 missed, and # 10 was a
possible hit.  Later identified ship as H.I.J.M.S. TAIGEI
class, ONI 14 P. 177).  He turned toward to ram, went deep
and he dropped five depth charges about a minute apart, the
first one exploding as we passed 180 feet, none of them were
close.

U.S.S. SILVERSIDES                   PATROL NO. 6
TORPEDO ATTACK No. 3
TIME 0107 K DATE August 30, 1943.   LAT 02-40 N.  LONG 150-00 E
TARGET DATA
DESCRIPTION
Original contact with target made at 1012 K
the previous day and had been stalking and tracking target
continuously since that time.  One day submerged and one
night submerged approach had been made previously but no
torpedoes fired as was not in favorable firing position.
At 0043, K, with target bearing 043 T, range 11,500 yds,
started surface approach.  Radar working well, two escorts
picked up at about 6000 yds, one on port bow, one on star-
board quarter.  Came in on a 50 starboard track, maintaining
a small silhouette and using slow speed to reduce wake.  Radar
ranges and TBT bearings were introduced into TDC about every
30 sec during approach and TDC was checking with plot.  At
0106 it appeared that we would not be able to sneak in much
further undetected so fired a spread of six torpedoes, using
a speed spread of 7.5 kts.  Fired six torpedoes at this target
because it was southbound, loaded and if previous rate of
prematures was maintained only three would cross the track.
Used depth setting of 18 feet to minimize chance of prematures.
No prematures were obtained and the tracks of three of the
torpedoes appeared to pass under the target.  It is believed
that he had a shallow draft and depth setting was too deep.
After attack, retired on the surface at 18 kts.
